# Interview prompts

Choose prompts that resolve the next decision. Do not administer the whole list at once. Be direct and curious; challenge a contradiction without arguing about taste.

## Household and priorities

- Who occupies the room on a typical evening, doing what, at the same time?
- Rank the top three activities. What is occasional rather than daily?
- How many proper seats are needed every day? What may count as a guest seat?
- What currently fails, and what would make the room feel successful six months later?
- Who else must agree? Where do their preferences conflict?
- Are there relevant mobility needs, children, pets, or routines that change reach, stability, cleaning, or clear-floor needs? Ask without inferring household composition from room labels.

Useful challenge: "You want a large chaise and open play space. Which deserves the best area every day, and which can be temporary?"

## Fixed items, scope, and money

- Which pieces must stay? Obtain dimensions, photos or model identifiers when their fit matters.
- Which walls, finishes, services, or fixtures may change? What must remain accessible?
- What is the comfortable budget, hard ceiling, currency, and included scope?
- Is this one purchase round or a phased plan? What is the deadline?
- Does the budget include delivery, installation, electrical work, curtains, and disposal, where applicable?

Useful challenge: "Keeping this sofa constrains the route. Is keeping it a hard requirement, a cost preference, or sentimental?"

## Activity-specific fit

- TV: essential or optional, screen size, main viewers, viewing habits, consoles/speakers, glare times, cables, and tolerance for a visible screen?
- Seating: upright conversation or lounging, preferred seat feel/depth, chaise/recliner use, and whether everyone needs a direct view?
- Entertaining: usual group size, food/drinks, movable seats, and the connection to dining?
- Play/exercise: the actual clear footprint and equipment; must it remain clear all day?
- Storage: enumerate what belongs here and how often it is accessed. Which items may remain visible?
- Light: when is the room used, where does sun/glare occur, and what lighting/controls exist?

## Taste with evidence

- Three desired feelings and three disliked features?
- Which specific feature in a reference works: layout, colour, texture, lighting, proportions, or styling?
- What do you dislike in an otherwise attractive reference?
- What level of visible cables, everyday clutter, maintenance, and material wear is acceptable?
- Would you choose the beautiful but less comfortable seat, or the comfortable but visually heavier one?

Photos from the entry and opposing corners, with windows, outlets, lights and existing furniture visible, can clarify the room when available. Ask for uploads in normal conversation rather than a text-only question tool. Do not block unrelated planning on missing photos.

## Feedback after options

- Which option would you actually live with? Which precise feature makes you hesitate?
- Which seat would each household member take?
- Walk mentally from the entrance to the kitchen and bedrooms. What happens when another person is seated or a door is open?
- What would you remove first if the room feels crowded?
- Which accepted decision should remain fixed next round, and what single uncertainty should we test?

End with a concise reading of the trade-off and a recommendation. Use the user's answer to adjust the brief; do not translate every reaction into a universal design rule.
